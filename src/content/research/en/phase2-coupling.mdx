Kilometre-scale weather models represent cities as roughness, not as individual buildings. For low-altitude UAV corridors that omission matters: street-scale shear and building wakes sit inside the same hour of mesoscale weather, and a single grid-cell wind cannot describe them.

This project builds an **offline, one-way WRF–OpenFOAM** pipeline over **downtown Guangzhou**. A kilometre-scale nested WRF run supplies hourly inflow; a building-resolving RANS solver (steady **k–ε**, one snapshot per hour) downscales the canopy-layer wind. The evaluation window is **September 2025**, covering about **two weeks** that include a **pre-typhoon regular period** and **one typhoon-affected case**. Winds are compared with **multi-station urban Doppler LiDAR** profiles at **several morphologically different urban sites**.

## Findings

The added value of building-resolving downscaling is **height-dependent**. Below about **300 m**, OpenFOAM reshapes the mesoscale wind toward the LiDAR profiles; above that layer, WRF and the downscaled field converge. How much a given site improves still **depends on local urban form**. Along representative low-altitude paths, an **open corridor and a dense-built corridor** that look similar in WRF show different wind exposure at **typical UAV flight heights**.

## Limits and next steps

Because the nesting is one-way, microscale skill remains bounded by the driving WRF field. The physics-based archive is also the basis for **faster, data-driven surrogates** aimed at operational urban wind products.
